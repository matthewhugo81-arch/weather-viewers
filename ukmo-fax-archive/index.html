Thickness & 850 hPa temperature guide

A quick UK air-mass guide for the dashed 1000–500 hPa thickness contours. Colours match the drawing pens; 1 dam = 10 metres.

Approximate associations: the 850 hPa ranges below are experience-based forecasting aids, not a validated conversion table or limits. Actual temperatures can fall outside them: thickness describes the whole layer, whereas 850 hPa samples one level.

ThicknessApprox. 850 hPa temperatureUK weather interpretation
564 dam+10 to +15°CWarm air mass. Can support hot weather in summer with sunshine and favourable surface conditions; thickness alone does not establish a heatwave. In winter, warmth aloft may be separated from colder surface air.
546 dam0 to +4°COften relatively mild maritime air, with rain favoured at low levels. Surface temperatures vary with cloud, wind and inversions; elevated terrain or a cold layer near the ground can alter precipitation type.
528 dam−7 to −5°CTraditional UK snow-risk indicator. Snow becomes more plausible, including at low levels when the lower atmosphere is cold enough. Maritime exposure, wet-bulb temperature and precipitation intensity affect marginal situations.
510 dam−15 to −10°CVery cold air mass with strong potential for low-level snow where precipitation occurs. Heavy snow showers require moisture and instability; settling depends on surface conditions and snowfall rate.
492 damBelow about −18°CExceptionally cold air mass. Severe frost and very cold daytime conditions are possible. Snowfall still needs moisture and lift; wind chill depends on the wind as well as temperature.

For precipitation type and settling, check the full temperature/wet-bulb profile, melting layers, elevation and ground conditions alongside these indicators.

Background: RMetS thickness guide · UK Weather Forecasts snow criteria · NWS thickness explanation. These references support the general principles, not the exact paired ranges above.

How to use this page
  1. Choose a forecast period, or use Previous / Next beside 4-up to move through the saved valid times. Current dates appear first; previous days remain under Earlier dates. The menu lists earlier forecast leads too: a saved T+84 may share a valid time with a newer T+72 and is then selectable in Chart B. T+24 means 24 hours after that chart's run time. The date alongside it is when the forecast is valid.
  2. Chart A is automatic: it always shows the newest saved run for that valid time.
  3. Chart B starts with the immediately previous run. Its menu lists older runs from newest to oldest. Both charts forecast exactly the same valid time.

The default combined view includes the Icelandic 00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC analyses (T+0) and T+24 forecasts, alongside the longer-range charts. These clock hours are cycle times, not T+6/T+12/T+18 forecast leads. An analysis can appear in A with earlier forecasts for that exact time in B once they have been saved.

Only one run saved? There is no earlier forecast to show yet. The hosted archive is checked hourly, on the hour (UTC), by GitHub once its workflow is enabled. Reload to see the latest published snapshot. For an offline copy only, run archive.bat to collect locally. Lead hours shown are the actual chart products; unavailable T+12 or T+18 products are not created.

Use Pen or a colour button to draw freehand on a chart. Red, green, blue and purple are available. Undo and Clear chart apply to the last chart you touched. Drawings stay in your browser for that exact image; other visitors see the original charts. Switch the pen off to scroll normally on touchscreens. Drawing is available in 2-up, Single and 4-up.

Single shows A only. 4-up adds the next older runs after your B selection. Wipe uses the compact image frame; Open original provides full resolution.
